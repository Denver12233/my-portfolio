## Overview

My first week at Makerspace InnovHub OPC marked the beginning of my journey as a backend intern. The week was a mix of onboarding activities, technical exploration, and hands-on implementation. Being assigned to the Backend Team under the supervision of the Assistant Tech Lead, I quickly realized that the team operates at a fast pace — by Day 4, I was already writing and connecting real database code.

## Day-by-Day

**February 9 — Orientation Day**

The week kicked off with a team orientation where our supervisor walked us through the company's structure and workflow. We were introduced to the tech stack that powers the project and interviewed for possible position assignments. It was a good first impression of how MakerSpace operates — structured, technical, and goal-oriented.

**February 10 — Exploring the PDRS and Agile Roadmap**

Our supervisor introduced the PDRS (Product Development Roadmap System) and the team's Agile workflow. Understanding how tasks are tracked and how sprints are structured gave me a clearer picture of how to approach my daily work and contributions within the team.

**February 11 — Tech Stack Exploration**

This day was dedicated to exploring the core technologies of the project. I spent time diving into the Next.js environment, setting up and exploring MongoDB as the database layer, and getting familiar with the SUI SDK and Piñata Storage. Each of these tools plays a specific role in the project's architecture, and understanding them early on was crucial before writing any actual code.

**February 12 — MongoDB Implementation**

With the exploration phase done, it was time to put things into practice. I implemented MongoDB for the database layer and successfully established a MongoDB connection within the Next.js application. Seeing the connection work end-to-end for the first time was a small but meaningful milestone in my first week.

**February 13 — Database Architecture: Role-Based Collections**

I worked on merging the separate user and admin databases into a single unified database collection. Each role was then assigned to its designated collection, making the database structure cleaner and easier to manage. This kind of architectural decision matters a lot in the long run for scalability and maintainability.

**February 14 — SeminarActivity and GitHub Setup**

The week wrapped up with participation in the SeminarActivity development task. As part of this, I created my GitHub account which will serve as the foundation for all version control and collaboration moving forward on the project.

## Key Takeaways

- Joined the Backend Team and got assigned under the Assistant Tech Lead at Makerspace InnovHub
- Learned the project's core tech stack: Next.js, MongoDB, SUI SDK, and Piñata Storage
- Successfully implemented and connected MongoDB to the Next.js application by Day 4
- Understood the importance of clean database architecture through the role-based collections task
- Set up GitHub for version control, which is essential for team collaboration
